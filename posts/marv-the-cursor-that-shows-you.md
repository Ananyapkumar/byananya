We've all been there. You know the setting exists. You've seen it before. And yet you're ten minutes deep into menus, opening every dropdown like it owes you money, before giving up and googling "where is the thing in the thing."

This week I found a tiny AI tool built for exactly that moment, and I honestly can't believe I hadn't heard of it sooner.

## Meet Marv

Marv is a small Windows app that sits right next to your cursor. When you're stuck, you hold a hotkey and just ask out loud: *where do I turn off notifications?* or *how do I freeze this row?*

Then it does the thing a good friend would do if they were sitting beside you. It **talks you through it out loud while drawing on your screen**. It circles the button. It points at the menu. It scribbles a note on top of whatever app you're in. It launched on Product Hunt in early October, and it's still flying well under the radar.

## Why I love it

**It meets you where you already are.** Most AI assistants make you leave what you're doing: switch tabs, type a long question, read a wall of text, switch back. Marv works on top of whatever's already open. Spreadsheet, design tool, some ancient admin panel your company refuses to replace. Because it works at the system level rather than inside one browser tab, it isn't tied to a single app.

**Pointing beats explaining.** When someone teaches you software in person, they don't hand you a paragraph. They lean over and go "there, that one." Marv copies that, and it's the part that made me smile. It's such a human way to help.

**It's quietly useful for everyone.** I immediately thought of three kinds of people: anyone learning a new tool for work, every parent who has ever fought their laptop settings, and me, every single time I open software I use twice a year.

## How did I miss this?

Turns out I'm a little late to a bigger shift. A Mac app called Clicky has been doing something similar since spring, and Google DeepMind has shown an experimental AI cursor that understands whatever you're pointing at. The cursor is quietly turning into the place where AI lives.

What I like about Marv is that it isn't trying to be everything. No "AI that runs your whole life." Just: *you're stuck, here's the button.* One problem, solved well.

## Small notes before you try it

- It's **Windows-only** for now, and still early.
- It looks at your screen to help, so check what it can see before using it around anything sensitive. That's good practice with any tool like this.

## My take

The AI tools I end up loving rarely feel like AI. They feel like someone being helpful at exactly the right moment, then getting out of the way. Marv is a tiny thing, and that's the point.
